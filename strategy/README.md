# Strategy

This area contains audience research, positioning choices, communication goals, and proposals that affect more than one campaign.

## Strategy document standard

Every strategy document should state:

- the problem being addressed;
- the primary audience and insight;
- the behavior or perception to change;
- the message and proof points;
- the channels and constraints;
- required people or resources;
- success and failure signals;
- the time horizon and review date;
- which parts are confirmed facts, interpretations, or proposals.

## Recommended strategy areas

- Developer acquisition and activation.
- User onboarding through functioning applications.
- Node operator and infrastructure communication.
- Ecosystem and builder storytelling.
- Press and analyst relations.
- Exchange, data-platform, and listing communication.
- Community contributor activation.
- International and language-specific growth.
- Reputation, incident, and misinformation response.

## Decision principles

- Lead with a real audience problem, not the token.
- Use one primary audience and one call to action per campaign.
- Prefer demonstrable utility over abstract claims.
- Reuse infrastructure and source material across channels.
- Keep strategy separate from campaign execution and published copy.
- Choose measurements connected to adoption, learning, or trust rather than likes alone.
